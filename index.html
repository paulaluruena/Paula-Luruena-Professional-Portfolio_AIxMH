Focus areas

Three things AI in mental health has to get right. Each one comes from something I’ve seen first-hand.

Grounded in how care works

Tools should reflect real clinical practice, not just what is easy to automate.

Watching clinicians run intake interviews and diagnostic evaluations showed me how much judgment sits behind every step. Good tools support that judgment rather than pretend to replace it.

Questions I’d explore

  • Which parts of care can technology make easier without losing the clinical reasoning behind them?
  • How should digital tools be evaluated before people rely on them?

Built on

Riverway Psychological HealthLearning & Brain Development Lab

Trust has to be earned

People share their most personal moments with these tools.

On a crisis line, what helped most was calm, clarity, and a fast route to the right person. Digital tools need the same: honest limits, careful privacy, and a quick hand-off to a human when it matters.

Questions I’d explore

  • How should a tool recognize a crisis and connect someone to real help quickly?
  • What makes someone trust, or stop trusting, a mental health app?

Built on

Cruz RojaAI & Society Club

Care in every language

Support only works if it reaches people where they are.

At Puentes, helping Hispanic families meant paying attention to immigration, work, and school, not only to the immediate request. Tools built for one culture can quietly leave everyone else behind.

Questions I’d explore

  • How do language and culture change what “helpful” looks like?
  • How can digital support reach communities that traditional care often misses?
