01 · Focus

Where psychology can make AI safer and kinder.

AI tools are increasingly part of how people look for mental health support. I want to help make sure they are built with an understanding of how people actually think, feel, and seek help. These are my areas of interest, grounded in the experience below.

01

Evidence first

Tools should reflect how assessment and therapy really work, not just what is easy to automate.

Grounded in clinical observation at Riverway and research at Tulane’s Learning & Brain Development Lab.

02

Trust & safety

People share their most personal information with these tools. Privacy, bias, and knowing when to hand off to a human all matter.

Grounded in crisis support at Cruz Roja and discussions on bias, privacy, and human–AI interaction in the AI & Society Club.

03

Culture & access

Support should work in someone’s own language and reflect their circumstances, so it reaches people who are often underserved.

Grounded in bilingual community work with Hispanic families at Puentes New Orleans.

Questions I keep coming back to

  • How can AI make mental health support easier to reach while keeping the person at the center?
  • What makes someone trust, or stop trusting, a digital mental health tool?
  • How should a tool recognize a crisis and connect someone to a human quickly?
  • How do language and culture change what “helpful” looks like?
